## Problem Statement

The founder reports that employees sometimes forget to check in or out. Attendance and shift records in spreadsheets are hard to reconcile into salary calculations, while project updates in WhatsApp are incomplete. The founder is building Attenda because their own office experiences these issues. There is no validated evidence yet that outside organizations will adopt it.

## Demand Evidence

No external users, paying customers, or adoption evidence were reported. The current signal is a firsthand problem in the founder’s office, not market validation. Missing attendance records can affect employee pay; frequency, correction time, and financial impact have not been measured.

## Status Quo

Attendance, shifts, and payroll are handled in Excel. Project updates are shared in WhatsApp groups, with some updates missing and no complete project tracking. The exact HR/payroll owner and the number or cost of corrections are still unknown.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

Initial workflow to validate: employees record attendance against their assigned shift; employees can submit a correction when a punch is missing; a manager/HR reviewer resolves it with a reason and history; approved records feed payroll preparation without silent overrides. The primary operator is the HR/payroll person who reconciles records, but their identity and role remain to be confirmed. Employees are directly affected when records are missing or wrong.

## Constraints

- Review existing work only; do not broaden into payment/billing.
- Preserve all pre-existing changes in the three repositories.
- Trace behavior consistently through API, web and mobile.
- Payroll-impacting decisions need visible sources, approvals and audit history; flags must not silently dock pay.
- Distinguish repository evidence from production behavior. No production access or live customer evidence was provided.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Attendance-to-payroll deep dive

Trace missed and offline punches, shift interpretation, corrections/reviews, permissions, employee-facing status, payroll inputs and cross-platform consistency through all three repositories. This gives the highest depth on the known employee-pay risk but omits other changed modules.

### Approach B: Equal-weight audit of every changed module

Review all work across all repositories at similar depth. This maximizes breadth but may produce an undifferentiated backlog before Attenda has validated a first adoption wedge.

### Approach C: Full feature audit, risk-prioritized

Review every changed feature area across all three repositories, including cross-feature associations, permissions, UI/UX and backend behavior. Start with attendance-to-payroll, then prioritize other issues by user impact, data integrity, security and operational risk.

## Recommended Approach

Approach C, selected by the user after clarifying that the full broad review is required. It covers the whole changed product surface while giving attendance-to-payroll first priority. The review must identify code-backed gaps, distinguish implementation status from proven live behavior, and keep findings grouped by feature and cross-feature dependencies.

## Open Questions

- Which of the named office contacts owns HR/payroll reconciliation, and what is their role?
- How often are punches missed, how long do corrections take, and how many salary records are changed as a result?
- Do outside organizations have the same workflow and would they switch or pay for it?
- Which devices and offline conditions are common in the intended customer segment?

## Success Criteria

- Review notes cover all changed feature areas across API, web and mobile, with source files/endpoints for each finding.
- Attendance-to-payroll is reviewed first and receives explicit cross-platform consistency and employee-pay risk analysis.
- Findings distinguish confirmed bugs, missing behavior, partial work, and unverified production assumptions.
- Review covers employee/HR/manager/owner paths across attendance, shifts, approvals, leave, remote work, overtime, payroll, projects/tasks, AI, notifications, permissions/settings and integrations, plus shared data and notification associations.
- High-risk attendance/payroll cases include missed punches, offline replay, breaks, overnight/location timezones, leave/shift conflict, correction approval, stale payroll inputs, retries/idempotency and concurrent actions.
- Role/permission behavior and employee-visible status are checked at each handoff.
- Each finding has impact, evidence, severity, feature association and a recommended next step; unrelated existing code is not changed.
- The founder observes the HR/payroll owner reconciling one real attendance/payroll period and records missed-punch frequency and correction effort before broadening the product scope.
